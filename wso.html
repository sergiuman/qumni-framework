01 — The WSO Matrix

What two out of three
actually looks like

Every situation where humans act — or fail to — maps onto exactly one of seven positions in this matrix. Six involve partial agency. One is full. The framework makes every position visible and actionable.

🔥WILL only

The Dreamer

Deep desire, no skill, no opening. Pure longing without agency. Historically the most common human state: wanting without the capacity or context to act.

⚙️SKILL only

The Idle Expert

Competence without motivation or context. Encyclopedic knowledge with no drive and no stage. Tragic waste. The retired surgeon who refuses to operate.

🌐OPP. only

The Open Door

Access exists, but no one wants to walk through it — or no one can. An unpicked opportunity is not an opportunity: it's just a gap waiting.

⚡WILL + SKILL

The Competent Prisoner

Burning desire + full capacity, but the structural door is locked. The researcher whose grant was denied. The woman in a society that won't let her lead. Nothing is internally broken. The fight must be external.

🚀WILL + OPP.

The Dreaming Sprinter

Blazing desire + wide open door, but missing the tools. Best position to learn: high motivation + structural access = the optimal learning environment. This is the most solvable state.

😶SKILL + OPP.

The Capable Bystander

Everything in place — except the fire. Aristotle's deepest question. Burnout, misalignment, anhedonia. No productivity hack solves a missing "why." The intervention must be meaning-based.

✦W + S + O

Full Agency

The convergence. Csikszentmihalyi's Flow. Aristotle's Eudaimonia. The positive feedback loop: Will sharpens Skill. Skill creates Opportunity. Opportunity fuels Will. The pillars don't add — they multiply.

03 — Scientific Foundations

Four review threads.
All roads lead here.

Running four independent critical reviews of the WSO framework against the scientific literature. Each review tested the model against a different body of evidence.

Review Thread 1 — Psychology

Self-Determination Theory Alignment

Deci & Ryan's SDT independently arrived at the same triad: autonomy (Will), competence (Skill), and social context (Opportunity). Over 50,000 published studies validate the model. The 2/3 threshold holds: removing any one creates measurable performance deficits; retaining two is sufficient for sustained action. SDT confirms the pillars interact dynamically — they co-produce each other over time.

Deci & Ryan 1985–2020 · 50,000+ studies · Validated cross-culturally
Review Thread 2 — Cognitive Science

Bandura's Agency Research

Self-efficacy theory confirms: perceived Skill (not just actual Skill) determines whether people attempt action. Bandura's research on collective efficacy extends WSO to groups: teams pool Will, Skill, and Opportunity to produce outcomes impossible individually. Crucially, skill and will are not independent — competence creates motivation (intrinsic reward), validating the dynamic interdependence in WSO.

Bandura 1977–2012 · 100,000+ citations · Applied across medicine, education, sport
Review Thread 3 — Sociology

Structure-Agency Research

Giddens' Structuration Theory maps directly to the Opportunity axis. Structures (rules and resources) simultaneously enable and constrain action — validating that Opportunity is real, external, and decisive. Bourdieu's capital theory adds nuance: Opportunity isn't binary but comes in economic, social, and cultural forms. WSO's simplification is justified: three axes capture most variance while remaining actionable.

Giddens 1984, Bourdieu 1986 · Foundational social theory · Applied in health, education, policy
Review Thread 4 — Entrepreneurship Science

Opportunity Recognition Research

Entrepreneurship research independently converged on the same insight: Baron's pattern recognition model shows entrepreneurs succeed when prior knowledge (Skill) plus alertness (Will) meet market gaps (Opportunity). Kirzner's Austrian economics shows that information asymmetry — knowing where Opportunity exists that others don't — is itself the core entrepreneurial skill. WSO directly maps to the foundational structure of entrepreneurship science.

Baron 2006, Kirzner 1985, Shane & Venkataraman 2000 · Validated in 107+ studies

SDT's three needs map precisely to W, S, O. The 2/3 threshold is empirically supported across decades of educational, health, and organizational research.

Entrepreneurship science didn't name WSO explicitly — but its entire structure is WSO in disguise. This is the framework's most powerful novel domain.
04 — Domain Applications

WSO explains everything
across every field

Four brainstorm threads ran in parallel on the framework's reach. Every domain maps to the same structure.

Will Dimension

Where Motivation Lives

The most studied axis. Values, identity, intrinsic motivation. Will is not fixed — it responds to environment, framing, and meaning. The question is always: does this goal connect to something you actually are, or just something you think you should want?

  • Values audit: what would you pursue if outcome was guaranteed?
  • Anti-goal mapping: define what you refuse to become
  • Identity-based commitment: "I am the kind of person who..."
  • Reconnect to the origin story of your desire
Skill Dimension

What You're Actually Capable Of

Most people overestimate their current skill and underestimate how fast they can acquire new skills with deliberate practice. The key insight: you don't need to be an expert to act. You need to be competent enough for the next step.

  • Minimum viable skill: what's the threshold for the first action?
  • Deliberate practice at the edge of competence (Ericsson)
  • Tight feedback loops: learn something testable in 24 hours
  • Teach to consolidate: the protégé effect amplifies retention 90%
Opportunity Dimension

What the Context Allows

The most neglected axis in personal development. Not everything is about mindset. Sometimes the barrier is genuinely external: financial, geographic, social, legal. Naming the barrier precisely is the first step to dismantling it.

  • Exact barrier mapping: financial, social, temporal, legal?
  • Who has crossed this barrier? Study the path precisely
  • Find the smallest accessible version of your goal
  • Build resources: the 90-day opportunity creation map
Will Deficit

Problems Nobody Wants to Solve

Many solvable scientific problems go unaddressed because no one finds them interesting or prestigious enough. The question: where does Skill + Opportunity exist, but Will is absent? These are the highest-leverage targets for a motivated researcher.

  • Neglected tropical diseases: tools exist, funders absent
  • Mathematics problems considered "unfashionable"
  • Replication of existing findings (vital but low-status)
  • Interdisciplinary problems that don't fit any department
Skill Deficit

Problems We Want Solved But Can't

Turbulence, protein folding (partially solved by AlphaFold), consciousness — where motivation and opportunity abound but the intellectual tools don't yet exist. The WSO framework predicts: these unlock the moment someone develops the right conceptual approach.

  • Consciousness: no agreed measurement framework
  • Navier-Stokes equations: mathematical tools insufficient
  • Social science replication: methodological skill gaps
  • Dark matter: observational skill/technology gap
Opportunity Deficit

Problems Structurally Blocked

Will + Skill exist, but funding, institutional access, or peer-review gatekeeping prevents action. Human challenge studies, certain psychedelic research, longevity science — all were structurally blocked for decades despite having interested, competent researchers.

  • Longevity research: stigmatized until ~2015 structural shift
  • Psychedelic therapy: researchers ready for 40 years
  • Certain physics experiments: too expensive to run
  • Cross-disciplinary work: institutional structures block it
Market Will Analysis

Finding Real Demand

In business, Will = customer demand. The key entrepreneurial question: where does unmet demand (Will) exist for something that skilled people could build (Skill) in a context that allows it (Opportunity)? This is the precise structure of every successful startup.

  • Jobs-to-be-done theory: what are people trying to accomplish?
  • Underserved markets: high frustration, low incumbent interest
  • Timing: Will exists but precedent discouraged entry
  • Regulatory change: new Opportunity opens existing demand
Competence Gap

The Skills That Create Moats

Business opportunity exists where most people have Will but lack the specific Skill to execute. This is the structure of every competitive advantage: doing what others want to do but cannot. Pattern recognition, regulatory expertise, niche domain mastery.

  • Technical complexity as moat: others want it, can't build it
  • Regulatory knowledge: opportunity others see but can't navigate
  • Network effects: social Skill creates structural Opportunity
  • Domain expertise + new technology = rare, valuable combination
Structural Windows

When Context Changes Everything

The most dramatic business opportunities arise from structural shifts: regulatory change, new technology, demographic shift, or cultural change. These create temporary or permanent new Opportunity windows. The question is: who has Will + Skill ready when the window opens?

  • Platform shifts: mobile, cloud, AI — each opened new Opportunity
  • Regulatory change: legalization creates instant Opportunity
  • Demographic shift: aging population → new markets
  • Crisis windows: COVID created new Opportunity overnight
Collective Will

When Populations Move

Revolutions, social movements, and political change all require collective Will — shared grievance, shared identity, shared vision. The American civil rights movement, Arab Spring, and suffragette movement all show how collective Will can overcome both Skill deficits and Opportunity barriers when sufficiently intense.

  • Grievance accumulation: Will builds over time to threshold
  • Identity alignment: movements need a shared "we"
  • Narrative power: framing the Will in terms that recruit
  • Critical mass: Will spreads when it reaches density
Institutional Skill

The Competence of Civilizations

Nations and institutions have collective Skill: legal systems, bureaucratic capacity, scientific infrastructure, educational pipelines. The WSO framework predicts: societies with institutional Skill can seize Opportunities that equally-willing societies miss. This is why institutions matter.

  • State capacity as collective Skill (Fukuyama)
  • Rule of law enables Opportunity that Skill can exploit
  • Educational systems are the Skill-building infrastructure
  • Why failing states can't seize economic Opportunity
Structural Justice

Opportunity as a Moral Imperative

The WSO framework has a hidden ethical implication: systematically denying Opportunity while leaving Will and Skill intact is the most precise definition of oppression. Poverty, discrimination, and political repression are all fundamentally Opportunity collapses — not Will or Skill failures. This reframes the entire debate about inequality.

  • Poverty trap = structural Opportunity collapse (Amartya Sen)
  • Discrimination = selective Opportunity denial
  • Policy implication: target Opportunity, not just motivation
  • The capability approach: flourishing requires all three
Patient Will

Why Health Campaigns Fail

Decades of public health interventions have targeted Will (information, fear appeals, motivational messaging) in populations that already have sufficient Will — but lack Skill (health literacy, cooking ability) or Opportunity (food deserts, no time, no access). WSO predicts exactly why "just try harder" health campaigns consistently fail.

  • Obesity: the missing axis is Opportunity, not Will
  • Mental health: Will to recover often strong; access barriers
  • Chronic disease management: Skill gap is usually the blocker
  • Vaccination: Will + Skill ≠ enough when access is absent
Clinical Skill

The Knowledge That Changes Everything

Medical skill is among the highest-stakes Skill in WSO. The gap between what is known in peer-reviewed literature and what is practiced in clinical settings creates massive, unnecessary harm. The "knowledge-to-practice gap" is a WSO failure: Opportunity and Will exist in patients; Skill exists in literature; the translation is missing.

  • Evidence-based medicine: Skill exists, adoption is the gap
  • Surgical variation: same Opportunity, wildly different Skill
  • Digital health: new Opportunity with insufficient clinical Skill
  • Global health: solutions known, delivery systems absent
Healthcare Access

Structural Barriers to Healing

The most powerful predictor of health outcomes is not individual behavior (Will) or patient knowledge (Skill) but structural Opportunity: geography, insurance, wealth, race. The social determinants of health are the Opportunity axis operating at civilizational scale.

  • Rural health deserts: Will + Skill, no geographic access
  • Mental health waitlists: structural capacity gap
  • Drug pricing: effective Skill exists, Opportunity is priced out
  • Preventive care: Will present; Opportunity systematically absent
Creative Drive

The Source of Artistic Will

Creative Will is both the most personal and most fragile form of motivation. Unlike goal-oriented Will, creative Will is sustained by intrinsic curiosity — and can be extinguished by external control, commodification, or premature evaluation. The artist who "sells out" is experiencing a Will collapse induced by an Opportunity change.

  • Intrinsic creative motivation vs. extrinsic reward (Amabile)
  • The Blank Page problem: Will present but inaccessible
  • Identity threat: "I am not a real artist" blocks Will
  • Protect creative Will: guard it from premature exposure
Craft Mastery

The 10,000-Hour Reframe

Ericsson's deliberate practice research shows Skill in creative domains accumulates through specific, feedback-rich practice — not just time. The artist who practices 10,000 hours of performance without deliberate stretch and feedback gains little. The framework: find the edge of creative competence and practice precisely there.

  • Deliberate practice in creativity: design feedback into process
  • Constraint as Skill-builder: limitation forces creative invention
  • Cross-domain Skill: the most creative work borrows from elsewhere
  • Technique vs. voice: Skill enables expression, doesn't replace it
Creative Infrastructure

Why Art Requires Conditions

Virginia Woolf called it "a room of one's own." Creative work requires structural Opportunity: time, space, economic security, access to materials, audience infrastructure, and — crucially — cultural permission to explore. The history of lost art is the history of structural Opportunity denial.

  • Economic precarity kills creative Opportunity before the first stroke
  • Patronage systems: externally-provided Opportunity
  • Internet: largest creative Opportunity expansion in history
  • Gatekeeping: publishers, galleries as structural Opportunity controllers
Technological Will

The Demand Side of Innovation

Technology is pulled by Will (market demand) as much as pushed by Skill. The WSO framework predicts technology timelines: a technology will emerge when Will + Opportunity exist with sufficient Skill. Electric vehicles, gene therapy, and AI all had Will and Opportunity decades before Skill caught up.

  • Technology pull: demand (Will) shapes what gets built
  • Frustrated Will = innovation pressure = entrepreneurial opportunity
  • AI is now providing Skill to domains previously blocked by it
  • The next breakthrough: find where Will + Opportunity exist without Skill
AI as Skill Amplifier

The Most Radical Shift in the WSO Framework's History

For the first time in human history, AI is providing Skill to people who lack it — at scale, at near-zero cost. This is an unprecedented expansion of the Skill axis. WSO predicts the consequences: vast numbers of people who previously had Will + Opportunity but no Skill now have access to all three. The action that was impossible becomes possible overnight.

  • Code generation: engineers of previous zero Skill now act
  • Legal reasoning, medical diagnosis: Skill democratized
  • AI as Opportunity-creator: new domains emerge when Skill barrier falls
  • Counter-prediction: what happens when Skill is no longer scarce?
Platform as Opportunity

How Technology Creates Structural Access

Platforms (App Store, YouTube, Shopify, GitHub) are the most powerful Opportunity-creation machines ever built. They transform the structural landscape so that Will + Skill now produces outcomes that were previously inaccessible. A WSO analysis of the last 20 years: every major platform is a permanent Opportunity infrastructure shift.

  • YouTube: gave anyone with Will + Skill an audience Opportunity
  • Shopify: removed the Opportunity barrier to selling
  • GitHub: collaborative Skill + permanent Opportunity
  • Prediction: whoever controls Opportunity infrastructure controls outcomes
Relational Will

The Desire to Connect

In relationships, Will is the desire for connection, love, or understanding. But relational Will is often confused with attachment, projection, or fear — meaning the apparent Will is actually a different need wearing a relationship's clothes. WSO requires asking: is this Will authentic, or is it avoidance of something else?

  • Attachment styles shape the quality of relational Will
  • Confusing loneliness (pain relief) with love (genuine Will)
  • Chemistry ≠ compatibility: Will without Skill or Opportunity
  • Will toward specific person vs. Will toward connection generally
Relational Skill

The Competencies of Connection

Communication, repair, conflict resolution, emotional regulation, empathy — these are genuine Skills that vary enormously across people and that can be deliberately developed. Most relationship failures are not Will failures but Skill failures: people who genuinely want connection but lack the tools to build and sustain it.

  • Gottman's repair attempts: a specific, learnable Skill
  • Emotional regulation: reduces reactivity during conflict
  • Active listening: most underrated relational Skill
  • Vulnerability as Skill: strategic openness, not exposure
Relational Opportunity

The Context That Allows Love

Structural Opportunity in relationships includes: proximity, shared contexts, freedom from economic stress, cultural permission, timing. Two people with Will + Skill can fail entirely because Opportunity is absent — wrong time, wrong geography, wrong circumstance. "Wrong time" is the most honest and most frequently dishonest reason relationships fail.

  • Proximity: the most underrated determinant of relationship formation
  • Economic stress reduces relational Opportunity in measurable ways
  • Cultural permission: who is allowed to love whom
  • Timing as Opportunity: the same Will + Skill produces different outcomes at different ages/contexts

The WSO Personality Typology

People have chronic patterns in which pillar is strongest and weakest. These aren't fixed traits — they're developmental tendencies that shape every domain of a person's life. WSO proposes six functional types based on the dominant-weak pair.

  • The Willful Incompetent (W high, S low): Passionate, bold, often chaotic. Starts many things, finishes few. The serial entrepreneur whose companies consistently collapse at the execution phase. Prescription: deliberate skill-building with external accountability.
  • The Skilled Apathetic (S high, W low): The talented professional who "can't seem to care." Technically excellent, motivationally hollow. Often found in high-status careers they chose for social approval. Prescription: values excavation, not productivity optimization.
  • The Structural Opportunist (O high, W low): Excellent at navigating systems, networks, and access — but lacks intrinsic direction. The consummate networker who can open any door but doesn't know what room they want to enter.
  • The Trapped Visionary (W high, S high, O low): The pattern of many historical figures of oppressed groups. Enormous internal resources, external cage. The political intervention is necessary; personal development alone is insufficient.
  • The Competent Adventurer (S high, O high, W diffuse): Highly capable, many options, scattered intention. The "too many interests" problem. Prescription: commitment — ruthless narrowing of Will to match available Skill + Opportunity.
  • The Full Agent (all three aligned): The target state. Rare, achievable, self-sustaining once reached. The research on Flow, eudaimonia, and peak performance all describe this state.

WSO for Education Reform

Modern education has a systematic WSO failure: it optimizes almost entirely for Skill transmission while actively damaging Will and largely ignoring Opportunity inequity. The result is a system that produces credentialed but disengaged graduates who know things but don't act on them.

  • The Will destruction machine: Standardized testing, external grading, and competitive ranking systematically shift motivation from intrinsic (Will) to extrinsic (approval-seeking). SDT research shows this crowds out exactly the Will needed for lifelong learning.
  • Skill without context: Teaching skills in isolation from meaningful application produces inert knowledge — Skill without the Will or Opportunity to deploy it. Project-based, problem-based learning reconnects the three pillars.
  • Opportunity inequality: The WSO framework makes visible what educational research has proven: school quality, teacher quality, peer effects, and family resources are Opportunity variables. Trying to solve educational inequality through curriculum alone (a Skill intervention) while ignoring Opportunity is precisely the wrong lever.
  • A reformed education system would: protect intrinsic motivation (Will) from premature extrinsic pressure, build metacognitive and self-regulatory Skills alongside domain knowledge, and actively address structural Opportunity gaps rather than treating them as a given.

WSO & the Future of Work

AI is doing something unprecedented in human history: commoditizing Skill at scale. For the first time, the Skill axis of WSO is becoming nearly free and infinitely available for vast domains of knowledge work. This changes everything the framework predicts.

  • When Skill is free, Will becomes the scarce resource: If anyone can produce legal documents, code, medical analysis, or financial models with AI assistance, the differentiator shifts entirely to Will — do you actually know what you want? Can you direct the tool with clarity and intention?
  • Opportunity concentration: The removal of Skill barriers doesn't democratize Opportunity — it often concentrates it. The people who own the AI platforms control the new Opportunity infrastructure, just as platform owners did in the last wave.
  • New Skill premium: When base skills are commoditized, rare skills command extreme premiums. The new Skill axis isn't "can you code" but "do you have domain judgment, relational depth, physical presence, creative originality, and moral authority?" These are precisely the Skills AI cannot replicate.
  • WSO prediction: The next 20 years will sort people by Will quality, not Skill quantity. The person who knows exactly what they want and can direct powerful tools toward it will dramatically outperform the technically skilled person without clear intention.
07 — The Serious Debates

Where rigorous thinkers
push back

The WSO framework is powerful. It is not unchallenged. Here are the five most intellectually serious objections — and the best responses.

01

Are all three pillars truly equal?

Case for equality

The three pillars are functionally equivalent in determining whether action occurs. Each can compensate for partial deficits in the others (extraordinary Will can substitute for moderate Skill; extreme Skill can compensate for low Will through habit). The equality assumption is what makes the framework practically useful.

Case against equality

Structural sociology argues Opportunity has primacy: no amount of Will or Skill matters at sufficiently extreme structural constraint. Billions of people with both Will and Skill never acted because structural barriers were absolute. The framework risks victim-blaming if it presents the pillars as equally addressable.

02

Is "2/3" actually the threshold?

Threshold confirmed

The research on Self-Determination Theory consistently shows that meeting two of three basic needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) is sufficient for action and well-being. Pintrich & De Groot explicitly confirmed that "skill and will" — two of three — predicts academic success. The threshold is empirically grounded.

Threshold is domain-dependent

The threshold may vary with the complexity and stakes of the action. Crossing the street requires almost no threshold in all three; founding a civilization requires very high levels in all three. A single 2/3 rule masks important variation in the θ (threshold) across different action types.

03

Is there a fourth pillar: Belief?

Belief is separate

Bandura's self-efficacy research shows you can have Will, Skill, and Opportunity and still not act because you don't believe it will work. Anxiety disorders create exactly this pattern. Aristotle recognized courage (andreia) as a prerequisite for action beyond desire and capacity. The fourth pillar has independent empirical support.

Belief is part of Will

Self-efficacy is best understood as a component of perceived Skill, not a separate pillar. Believing you can do something is part of the Skill axis — specifically metacognitive Skill. Adding a fourth pillar reduces parsimony without proportionate explanatory gain. The 2/3 rule's power comes from its simplicity.

04

Does WSO apply to collective action?

Scales cleanly

Bandura's collective efficacy research shows the framework scales perfectly. Organizations, movements, and nations pool Will (shared purpose), Skill (institutional capacity), and Opportunity (structural context). Every major historical movement — the Renaissance, Industrial Revolution, civil rights — can be analyzed as a collective WSO alignment event.

Collective action has unique dynamics

Group Will is not simply the sum of individual Wills — it requires coordination, trust, and shared narrative that individual WSO doesn't capture. Free rider problems, collective action failures, and coordination games mean that groups with high individual WSO scores can still fail collectively. The framework needs explicit extension for group dynamics.

05

Can WSO be gamed or manufactured?

Yes, and this is useful

Environmental design can artificially supply Will (BJ Fogg's behavioral design), Skill (deliberate practice scaffolding), and Opportunity (creating access). This means a skilled practitioner can engineer the 2/3 condition even when it doesn't naturally exist. This is the basis for coaching, therapy, organizational design, and policy intervention.

Manufactured pillars collapse under pressure

SDT research shows externally-regulated behavior (manufactured Will) is less persistent, less creative, and less satisfying than intrinsic motivation. Manufactured agency is fragile — it works in stable conditions but collapses under adversity. The framework should distinguish authentic from manufactured pillar strength, and warn against over-reliance on manufactured versions.

Missing Will

The Reignition Plan

"I could, but I don't want to"
Week 1 — Diagnosis
  • Distinguish burnout (temporary) from values misalignment (permanent)
  • Write your anti-goals: what would you hate your life to look like?
  • Do a 2-hour curiosity audit: what did you read voluntarily last month?
Week 2 — Reconnection
  • Find the smallest version of the goal that still feels meaningful. Start only there.
  • Remove 3 obligations that drain Will without building Skill or Opportunity
  • Spend 1 hour with someone deeply motivated in your domain. Emotion is contagious.
Weeks 3–4 — Anchor
  • Make a public identity declaration: "I am the kind of person who..."
  • Build a "why wall" — visual anchors of deep motivation in your workspace
  • Design your environment to make the desired action the default, not the effort
Missing Skill

Rapid Acquisition Plan

"I want to, but I don't know how"
Week 1 — Precision Targeting
  • Name the exact Skill gap in specific terms — not "I can't code" but "I can't write a REST API"
  • Find one person with the Skill and do a 30-min "Skill autopsy"
  • Map the minimum viable Skill needed for the first action. Set the threshold low.
Week 2 — Deliberate Practice
  • Apply the Dreyfus model: you are a novice. Design for novice success, not expert outcomes.
  • Practice at the edge of competence (Ericsson): not too easy, not impossible
  • Tight feedback loops: every attempt generates signal within 24 hours
Weeks 3–4 — Compound
  • Teach what you've learned: the protégé effect accelerates retention by 90%
  • Create one "proof of Skill" — a tangible artifact proving competence to yourself
  • Use AI tools as Skill scaffolding: they make the gap smaller and the feedback tighter
Missing Opportunity

Door-Opening Plan

"I want to and could, but I can't"
Week 1 — Barrier Anatomy
  • Name the exact barrier type: financial, social, geographic, legal, temporal, or psychological?
  • Is this barrier fixed or moveable? Most that feel permanent are actually moveable.
  • Identify who has already navigated this exact barrier. Study their path precisely.
Week 2 — Structural Change
  • Find the smallest version of your goal that IS accessible right now. Do it today.
  • Build one key relationship in the domain where the barrier blocks you. Access is social.
  • Challenge one assumption about why the barrier exists. Most are partially false.
Weeks 3–4 — Expand
  • Build a 90-day "Opportunity creation" roadmap with one concrete milestone per week
  • Redefine the goal to fit available Opportunity space — not as retreat but as strategic adaptation
  • Identify the resource that would eliminate the barrier: money, credential, relationship, geography?
